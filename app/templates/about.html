Why this exists

There was a time when finding a record took effort. You went through crates, you asked people, you taped things off the radio at 2am hoping the DJ wouldn't talk over the intro. And because it took effort, you remembered exactly who put you onto what.

Now a machine queues the next track before the current one ends. It's good at it. Too good — after a while everything sounds like the last thing you played, and you couldn't name a single person responsible for any of it.

And when we do send each other music, it's a link in a group chat. No context. No "wait for what he does at 1:40". Just a link, and the other person hears a song instead of the thing you heard.

"Birthdays was the worst days, now we sip champagne when we thirsty."

Biggie, Juicy. Send the link and it's just a good song. Say "listen to what he does with the birthday line" and it becomes something else. That's the whole difference.

{# ---------- Le partage ---------- #}

Sharing is the point

Nobody found their favourite album alone. Someone's older brother played it. Someone's mate wouldn't shut up about it until you gave in. A producer sampled something and you spent a week tracking the original.

Every good record in your life has a person attached to it. Algorithms can't do that — they can only tell you what's similar. A person tells you what's worth your time, and why.

Why "digging"

Crate digging is going through records one by one until something stops you. Producers did it for samples, DJs did it for sets, and everyone else did it because that's how you found anything. It was slow, and that was the point — the work is what made the find yours.

"I came from no space, wasn't safe. Now it's like no space in a safe."

Fabolous. You either heard that and stopped the song, or you didn't. This is a place for the people who stopped the song.
